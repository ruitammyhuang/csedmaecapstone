Sprint 1: Define a Problem / a Wonder

D&D Projects: Define and Empathize

Tasks

Problem Identification

Write a concise problem statement addressing a practical issue in CS education. Focus on a challenge relevant to a specific learner population.

Describe Target Learners

Clearly define the learner population (e.g., grade level, background, learning needs). Explain why this population is important for your project.

Understand Stakeholders

Conduct quick consultations (e.g., informal interviews or surveys) with at least two stakeholders (students, teachers, administrators, etc.). Gather insights about their challenges, goals, and preferences. Provide de-identified support documents as evidence (e.g., anonymized interview or survey notes).

Theoretical Grounding

Link the problem to theories, frameworks, principles, or pedagogies from the program, explaining why these inform your project. Use 1-2 citations to support your rationale and provide evidence (e.g., articles, resources) showing why the problem is worth solving.

Define Success

Outline measurable success indicators for the project (e.g., improved engagement, enhanced accessibility, mastery of concepts).

Deliverables

  • A Problem Brief (1 page) including:
    • Problem statement
    • Description of target learner population and context
    • Summary of stakeholder insights (empathy findings)
    • Connection to theories/frameworks/pedagogies (with 1-2 citations)
    • Indicators of success
  • Supporting documents:
    • Anonymized interview or survey notes with stakeholders
    • Resources or evidence supporting the problem's importance

Please combine all required deliverables into one Google doc (add links to supporting documents if applicable), and then submit a sharable link to this discussion board. Make sure your peers can leave comments in your document.

Teacher Inquiry Projects: Define a Wonder

Tasks

Formulate Your Wonder

Develop an open-ended question that addresses a CS education-related challenge. Ensure the question is rooted in a practical issue from your professional experience.

Describe Target Learners

Define the learner population related to your inquiry, including their grade level, background, and educational context.

Provide Context

Briefly describe the teaching or learning environment where the inquiry will take place (e.g., classroom type, available technology, curriculum structure).

Theoretical Grounding

Connect your wonder to theories, frameworks, principles, or pedagogies from the program, explaining their relevance. Use 1-2 citations to strengthen your reasoning and provide evidence (e.g., articles, resources) supporting why your wonder is worth exploring.

Scope and Feasibility

Define the scope of your inquiry, focusing on a manageable aspect of your wonder that can be explored within the available timeframe.

Deliverables

  • An Inquiry Proposal (1 page) including:
    • The formulated wonder (question)
    • Description of the target learner population and context
    • Connection to theories/frameworks/pedagogies (with 1-2 citations)
    • Statement of feasibility and focus
  • Supporting documents:
    • Anonymized interview or survey notes with stakeholders
    • Resources or evidence supporting the wonder's importance

Please combine all required deliverables into one Google doc (add links to supporting documents if applicable), and then submit a sharable link to this discussion board. Make sure your peers can leave comments in your document.

Peer Review Requirement

Peer Review Tasks

Review at least one peer’s submission (Problem Brief or Inquiry Proposal) before our bi-weekly meeting.

  • Clarity and Relevance: Evaluate whether the problem statement or wonder is clearly stated and addresses a practical issue in CS education.
  • Learner Population: Assess whether the learner population is well-defined and appropriately contextualized.
  • Connection to Theories/Frameworks: Suggest additional theories, frameworks, or principles that could enhance the project or inquiry.
  • Feasibility and Focus: Provide feedback on the scope and practicality of the proposed work.
