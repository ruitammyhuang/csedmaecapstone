Ed Tech Tool D&D: Ideation Due Friday (before meeting)

▾

Generate and refine multiple ideas for an educational technology tool, then choose one direction to move forward.

Tasks

Complete these tasks early so you can draft a strong deliverable and get feedback on time.

  • Brainstorm multiple ideas
    Brainstorm at least three potential ideas for an educational technology tool addressing the problem identified in Phase 1.
  • Evaluate with constraints
    Consider stakeholder needs, usability, and alignment with CS education principles.
  • Refine with stakeholders
    Engage in informal discussions or consultations with stakeholders to refine ideas.
  • Select one direction
    Select one idea to move forward with, providing a rationale for the selection.
  • Visualize the idea
    Create mock-ups or conceptual diagrams to visualize the selected idea.
Deliverables

Submit by Friday so the instructor can review and provide formative feedback before we meet.

  • Ed Tech Tool Design Brief including:
    • Three initial ideas with brief descriptions of how each addresses stakeholder needs and aligns with CS education principles
    • A detailed explanation of the selected idea, including rationale and alignment with the problem identified in Phase 1
    • Mock-ups or diagrams of the selected idea
  • Supporting documents:
    • Notes from brainstorming sessions or discussions with stakeholders
    • References to resources or frameworks that inspired the ideation process

Combine everything into one Google Doc (link supporting documents if needed) and submit a shareable link to this Google Spreadsheets file under the corresponding worksheet.

Make sure your Google doc allows your peers to leave comments.

Teacher Inquiry Projects: Data Collection Plan Due Friday (before meeting)

▾

Plan what evidence you will collect, how you will collect it, and how you will protect participants and data.

Tasks

Complete these tasks early so you can draft a focused plan and get feedback on time.

  • Identify needed data
    Identify the types of data needed to explore your wonder effectively.
  • Select methods
    Select appropriate methods for data collection (for example, surveys, interviews, observations, or artifacts).
  • Draft instruments
    Develop instruments or tools needed for data collection (for example, survey questions, interview guides).
  • Build a timeline
    Create a timeline for data collection activities, ensuring feasibility within the sprint.
  • Address ethics and security
    Address ethical considerations, including how you will anonymize and secure participant data.
Deliverables

Submit by Friday so the instructor can review and provide formative feedback before we meet.

  • Data Collection Plan including:
    • A detailed description of the data to be collected and its relevance to your wonder
    • An explanation of the methods chosen for data collection
    • A timeline for data collection activities over the next sprint
    • A description of ethical considerations and steps for data anonymization and security
  • Supporting documents:
    • Drafts of any instruments or tools (for example, surveys, interview guides)
    • References to resources or examples that informed your data collection plan

Combine everything into one Google Doc (link supporting documents if needed) and submit a shareable link to this Google Spreadsheets file under the corresponding worksheet.

Make sure your Google doc allows your peers to leave comments.

Peer Review DUE SUNDAY (BEFORE MEETING)

Review at least one peer’s submission (Design Idea Brief or Data Collection Plan).

Focus areas
  • Clarity and alignment: Does the submission clearly connect to the Sprint 1 problem/wonder?
  • Creativity and feasibility: Are the ideas/plans ambitious but doable within the course timeline?
  • Connection to stakeholders: Does the submission demonstrate attention to stakeholder/learner needs?
  • Actionable suggestions: Offer specific, practical feedback that the author can implement next sprint.
