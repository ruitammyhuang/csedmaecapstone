D&D Projects: Define and Empathize Due Friday (before meeting)

▾

Design and Development (D&D) track includes two types of projects: design and develop tools for computer science education (for example, AI tools for learning programming language), or design and develop new CS course (for example, a new introductory programming course for your current or future teaching assignment).

Tasks

Complete these tasks early so you can draft a strong deliverable and get feedback on time.

  • Problem identification
    Write a concise problem statement addressing a practical issue in CS education. Focus on a challenge relevant to a specific learner population.
  • Describe target learners
    Clearly define the learner population (for example, grade level, background, learning needs). Explain why this population matters for your project.
  • Understand stakeholders
    Conduct quick consultations with at least two stakeholders (students, teachers, administrators, etc.). Gather insights about challenges, goals, and preferences. Provide de-identified evidence (for example, anonymized interview or survey notes).
  • Theoretical grounding
    Link the problem to theories, frameworks, principles, or pedagogies from the program. Use 1–2 citations and include evidence that the problem is worth solving.
  • Define success
    Outline measurable success indicators (for example, improved engagement, enhanced accessibility, improved mastery of concepts).
Deliverables

Submit by Friday so the instructor can review and provide formative feedback before we meet.

  • Problem Brief (about 1 page) including:
    • Problem statement
    • Description of target learner population and context
    • Summary of stakeholder insights (empathy findings)
    • Connection to theories/frameworks/pedagogies (with 1–2 citations)
    • Indicators of success
  • Supporting documents:
    • Anonymized interview or survey notes with stakeholders
    • Resources or evidence supporting the problem’s importance

Combine everything into one Google Doc (link supporting documents if needed) and submit a shareable link to this Google Spreadsheets file under the corresponding worksheet.

Make sure your Google doc allows your peers to leave comments.

Teacher Inquiry Projects: Define a Wonder Due Friday (before meeting)

▾

Teacher Inquiry track aligns with the course "EDE 6325 Guided Teacher Inquiry", and provides you an opportunity to explore a question related to your CS teaching practice.

Tasks

Complete these tasks early so you can draft a focused proposal and get feedback on time.

  • Formulate your wonder
    Develop an open-ended question addressing a CS education-related challenge rooted in your professional context.
  • Describe target learners
    Define the learner population related to your inquiry, including grade level, background, and educational context.
  • Provide context
    Briefly describe the environment where the inquiry will take place (for example, classroom type, available technology, curriculum structure).
  • Theoretical grounding
    Connect your wonder to theories, frameworks, principles, or pedagogies from the program. Use 1–2 citations and include evidence supporting why your wonder is worth exploring.
  • Scope and feasibility
    Define a manageable scope that can be explored within the available timeframe.
Deliverables

Submit by Friday so the instructor can review and provide formative feedback before we meet.

  • Inquiry Proposal (about 1 page) including:
    • The formulated wonder (question)
    • Description of the target learner population and context
    • Connection to theories/frameworks/pedagogies (with 1–2 citations)
    • Statement of feasibility and focus
  • Supporting documents:
    • Anonymized interview or survey notes with stakeholders
    • Resources or evidence supporting the wonder’s importance

Combine everything into one Google Doc (link supporting documents if needed) and submit a shareable link to this Google Spreadsheets file under the corresponding worksheet.

Make sure your Google doc allows your peers to leave comments.

Peer Review DUE SUNDAY (BEFORE MEETING)

Review at least one peer’s submission (Problem Brief or Inquiry Proposal).

Focus areas
  • Clarity and relevance: Is the problem statement or wonder clearly stated and grounded in a practical CS education issue?
  • Learner population: Is the learner population well-defined and appropriately contextualized?
  • Connection to theories/frameworks: What theories, frameworks, or principles could strengthen the project or inquiry?
  • Feasibility and focus: Is the scope manageable? What would make it more realistic or more focused?
